Kepler’s three laws

Two planets orbit one star. Each position is solved from Kepler’s equation, so a planet speeds up when it swings close to the Sun.

Law 1 Ellipses

Each planet travels on an ellipse with the Sun at one focus, not the center. The other focus is empty. From any point on the orbit, the distances to the two foci add up to 2a.

Law 2 Equal areas

In equal times, the line from the Sun to a planet sweeps out equal areas. The shaded wedges are planet 2 over the same tenth of its year: short and wide near perihelion, long and thin near aphelion.

Planet 2 is moving — its slowest speed.

Law 3 Periods

Square the time for one orbit, then divide by the cube of the semi-major axis a. The table above stays matched for both planets. Eccentricity changes the shape and the speed along the path. It does not change the ratio, or how long the year is.

Clock Time

Speed up the clock, or pause it. The inner planet finishes its year first, whatever the eccentricities are.
